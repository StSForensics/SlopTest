# SlopTest

Six free tests for checking a piece of writing for real argument, filler, story, facts that hold up, and whether any of it is creative. Paste your text on the page and it builds the prompt for you; you send it to the AI tool you already use. The page itself sends nothing to us.

Version 3.5 (beta), 20 November 2026. New in 3.5: Open switches off for text that looks confidential; invisible characters are stripped. New in 3.4: counts are done in code, Exhibit C asks for evidence receipts, scores ask for a counter-quote. Feedback welcome: SlopTestFeedback@o4m.ai. New: a paste box that builds ready-to-send prompts, a one-prompt Quick check (cannot award the top label) and a guided Full audit, with Copy and Open buttons, and an optional box for your own standard (company, teacher, expert or personal voice) that can only add checks or lower a score. The Open buttons put your text inside a link to the tool you pick, so it can appear in browser history; Copy does not. The whole tool is one file, `index.html`, so its source is the page itself. It loads nothing from other websites and sets no cookies.

Run order: Exhibit A (blueprint), C (fact check, needs web access), D (probable-path test), E (craft gates), then B (judge, in a fresh chat, ideally a different AI vendor from the one that wrote the piece), then F (three human readers).

What the scores are: the opinions of an AI system, written by the same kind of system that does the judging. They are a way to find weak sentences, not proof of quality or of truth. The page cites the research on why the Slop score is the least reliable of the four. The quote requirement, the fact gate and the human read in Exhibit F are the sturdier parts.
